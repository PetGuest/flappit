How to make a split-flap video in three steps

  1. Type your message. One or more lines of up to 13 characters. Short works best: a phrase, a name, a date, a “yes”.
  2. Hit Create video. The board spins for about 11 seconds in your browser. Nothing is uploaded anywhere: the video is made on your own phone or computer.
  3. Save or share. On a phone, the Share button sends it straight to TikTok, Instagram or WhatsApp. On a computer it downloads as a video file.

What people use it for

Anything that is better said by making people wait: a gender reveal, a trip announcement, a wedding date, a birthday, an “I love you” or a “we are moving”. And the everyday side too: the café’s menu of the day, this week’s offer, summer opening hours or Monday’s motivational line. An airport board that spins and clicks makes a three-word message feel like an event.

The trick is the choreography: the flaps spin at full speed and, towards the end, the letters stop one by one, in random order. Viewers try to guess the line before it finishes. That small mystery is what gets the video watched to the end, and on social, watched to the end is what counts.

Format tip. The video is vertical (1080×1920) and leaves the top and bottom strips free where TikTok and Instagram place their buttons, so your message is never covered. It lasts about 11 seconds: just enough for the algorithm to count it as watched through.

The split-flap board, from Solari to your phone

Flip-flap boards were invented by the Italian firm Solari in the 1950s for stations and airports. Their sound, that cascading clack-clack, is one of the few machine noises people remember fondly. Few real ones remain; we reproduce them in software with the same cadence and the same double click. The full story is in History of the split-flap display.
